RESOLVED — this answers the open question from the BM25/EB-NeRD entry above.
Semantic clearly beats chance here (AUC=0.5399) where BM25 did not
(AUC=0.4929, essentially random). Every metric is higher: MRR 0.3364 vs
0.3159, nDCG@10 0.4519 vs 0.4292. This is the OPPOSITE ordering from Q3's
full-corpus recall@K, where BM25 clearly beat semantic on EB-NeRD (4-8x).
Coherent explanation: BM25's query was deliberately shortened to title-only
text from the last 5 clicks (a speed trade forced by full-corpus scoring
cost, see ai_usage_log.md) and apparently doesn't carry enough signal for
fine-grained re-ranking of a small ~9-candidate set, even though it was
sufficient for the coarser "find it somewhere in 125K" task. Semantic's user
representation was never touched by that trade (full title+abstract text,
mean-pooled over the last 10 clicks) and retains just enough usable signal
to meaningfully beat chance at re-ranking, despite being far weaker at
full-corpus retrieval. Net takeaway for the design note: which method "wins"
depends entirely on which task you're evaluating — full-catalog candidate
generation (Q2/Q3 recall@K) vs. re-ranking an already-curated shortlist
(Q4's harness) are genuinely different problems that reward different
retrieval properties.

FINDING — counterintuitive, worth a paragraph in the design note: tail
(unpopular) articles rank *better* under BM25 than head (popular) ones —
AUC 0.5328 vs 0.4921, i.e. tail is clearly above chance while head is at
chance. This is the opposite of the usual "popularity bias favors head
items" story. Plausible mechanism: BM25's whole scoring signal is IDF —
rare, distinctive vocabulary. A niche/tail article is more likely to contain
uncommon terms that give it a sharp, discriminative BM25 score; a popular
head article (often mainstream, broadly-worded news) is more likely to share
generic vocabulary with many other candidates in the same impression, making
it harder for pure lexical overlap to tell them apart. This is a real
argument for why a production system would want semantic/behavioral signals
layered on top of BM25 specifically for head-item disambiguation, even
though BM25 alone already does reasonably at surfacing tail content.
